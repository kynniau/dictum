My assessment: 7.2 is a useful starting point, but insufficient as a conservative default. These are challenges for counsel’s sign-off, not conclusive legal advice.

- **Disagree — Commonwealth formulation (high confidence on statutory test; medium on implementation).** Being a participant is not itself the TIA exemption: ss 5F–6 turn on whether communication remains in passage and the sender’s knowledge. Endpoint recording appears supportable, but counsel should review Dictum’s actual audio-capture path. [TIA Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02124/2026-01-22/2026-01-22/text/original/pdf/1)

- **Disagree — Queensland omission (high confidence).** Section 43’s participant exception is correctly identified, but **s 45** separately restricts disclosure by lawful participant-recorders, including statements prepared from recordings; s 44 concerns unlawfully obtained conversations. Correct 7.2’s citation and assess internal sharing/transcripts. [Act, ss 43–45](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2024-12-31/act-1971-050)

- **Disagree — NSW completeness (high confidence).** Section 7(3)(b)(ii) also permits recording with one principal party’s consent where recording is not for communicating/publishing to nonparties; the draft omits this alternative. That does not establish permission for broker compliance workflows. [NSW Justice explanation](https://dcj.nsw.gov.au/documents/legal-and-justice/laws-and-legislation/surveillance-devices-act-2007-and-statutory-powers-to-make-audio-and-video-recordings.pdf)

- **Agree — Victoria, broadly (high confidence).** Sections 6 and 11 support the summary, but public/lawful-interest disclosure must be no more than reasonably necessary; these are qualified exceptions. [Current authorised Act](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/99-21aa048-authorised.pdf)

- **Agree — Philippines, with additions (high confidence).** RA 4200 covers participant recording, but also knowing possession, replay and transcription/disclosure of unlawfully obtained material; importing is therefore a substantive risk. [RA 4200](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/4442), [Ramirez](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_93833_1995.html)

- **Disagree — Privacy Act coverage (high confidence).** Credit-provider status does not automatically impose APPs on every recording; Part IIIA coverage is distinct, and s 6E’s AML/CTF extension is activity-specific. Separately assess broker/agent status, credit information, TFNs and APP coverage. [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business), [s 6E](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/2025-06-10/2025-06-10/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

- **Add — territorial coverage.** SA, Tasmania, WA, ACT and NT are acknowledged but unresolved: require a recording/use/disclosure matrix and interstate application analysis, including relevant external territories and overseas participants; “cannot know location” should not replace asking.

- **Add — missing privacy regimes.** Include Australia’s serious-invasion tort, operative since June 2025 even beyond APP entities, Philippine Data Privacy Act requirements, and India’s existing IT/privacy rules plus phased DPDP commencement. [OAIC tort](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/more-privacy-rights/statutory-tort-for-serious-invasions-of-privacy), [Philippine Act](https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act-/), [Indian government](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2190655&lang=2&reg=48)

- **Disagree — ASIC research implication (high confidence).** RG 273 expressly recognises audio recordings as compliance records without requiring universal recording; assess NCCP assessments, retention, retrieval, AFCA/IDR and litigation holds. Determine AML/CTF coverage from actual designated services, not “broker” alone. [RG 273.165–168](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/5641325/rg273-published-24-june-2020.pdf), [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/who-and-what-we-regulate)

- **Disagree — five decisions suffice.** Keep per-session agreement, no covert mode and no state switch, but an operator’s click/time proves acknowledgement, not everyone’s agreement; record participants, consent method/scope and script version, with refusal/manual-notes, withdrawal/stop and late-joiner handling.

- **Add — informed consent and lifecycle.** Explain recording, transcription, purposes, recipients/offshore access and retention before agreement; address capacity/language, sensitive information, security, access/correction and deletion. Local processing does not resolve overseas access obligations. [APP 8](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information)

- **Add — employment and consumer law.** Review staff-monitoring policies and NSW/ACT workplace-surveillance applicability; NSW’s notice regime is not a blanket audio rule. Check “local-only” and compliance claims for misleading representations. [NSW Act](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2005-47), [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims)

- **Add — counsel priorities.** Put capture architecture/concurrent jurisdictions, consent evidence, lawful imports and downstream uses first; then coverage, retention and employment controls, with wording last. Deprioritise NSW lawful-interests reliance if all-party agreement remains mandatory.
